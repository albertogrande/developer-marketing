# Reader Taste

Agent-maintained. Durable preferences only — update when the reader gives
feedback (in session or via issue comments); prune one-off reactions.

## Who the reader is

A **practitioner who markets to developers**: a developer marketer, a DevRel
lead, a product marketer for a dev tool, or a founder of a developer-first
company. Sophisticated — consumes a lot of dev-marketing content and hates
fluff. Wants the state of the art, kept honest.

## What the reader wants

- **Practical and testable.** A play, framework, metric, or habit they can put
  into practice this quarter — "gate your quickstart on time-to-first-call,
  not signups", "report DevRel in influenced pipeline, not attributed leads",
  "kill the gated PDF". A play they can test beats an observation.
- **Two jobs, not one.** (1) Stay current each week — the short weekly digest.
  (2) An always-current guide covering the whole field. Signals feed both;
  don't confuse the two.
- **Opinionated.** State a take in one plain sentence. Make calls. Hedge only
  when honestly uncertain, and say why.
- **Sourced.** Every claim traceable to a public, linkable source — the report
  itself, the operator's own write-up, the original thread. Distinguish
  independent evidence from a vendor's marketing. Prefer numbers you can source.
- **Dated facts over timeless advice — especially for practices.** A bare
  model already knows the timeless judgment (don't gate dev content, lead with
  utility, respect the audience); the corpus's edge is what's *current* — a
  survey wave, a channel shift, a measured pattern with a date on it. New
  practices earn their place by encoding what changed and when, not by
  restating good marketing sense.

## Voice

- Clarity above all: short sentences, simple words, short paragraphs. Depth
  from specifics — figures, company names, dated moves — not from rhetorical
  flourish. If a sentence needs reading twice, rewrite it.
- Inherited house style: what happened → why it matters → what to do about it.

## What bores the reader

- Padding a quiet day into a forced daily article — the reason the daily radar
  became signals + a weekly digest. A quiet stretch is fine; capture it and
  move on.
- Narrow, samey "what is DevRel" explainers with no practical payoff.
- Vendor puff repeated as insight — a case study is data, a press release is not.

## Format

- **Signals** (daily, internal) — raw one-liners, capture over synthesis.
- **The guide** (evergreen) — the product; kept continuously current.
- **The weekly** (one short essay a week) — decides what mattered; areas are
  research lenses, never output sections. Not a per-item news list.
- **Deep dives** — commissioned only when a thread earns it (see MEMORY.md),
  or on demand by topic.
- **Practices** (atomic, machine-readable) — when X → do Y (because Z), dated
  and sourced; distilled by the weekly editor.
