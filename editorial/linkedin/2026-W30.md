GitHub spent one week pricing AI code review — and then shipped the dashboard
that tells its own customers which Copilot seats they're wasting.

Those two moves belong together, and they say a lot about how AI devtools get
sold from here. That's the argument of this week's issue of The Week.

What moved:

• GitHub Code Quality went GA: $10 per active committer, plus a usage meter on
the AI parts. Seat + meter is now the default price shape for AI devtools, not
an experiment. https://github.blog/changelog/2026-07-20-github-code-quality-is-now-generally-available/

• Two days later, GitHub's new Copilot impact dashboard sorts enterprise seats
into adoption cohorts — including a named "Passive (licensed but not engaged)"
segment. The seller of the seats built the tool that counts the buyer's
shelfware. https://github.blog/changelog/2026-07-22-new-copilot-usage-metrics-impact-dashboard/

• Why would a vendor do that? A 500-enterprise HCLTech survey
(vendor-commissioned — grade it accordingly) says 90% of decision-makers call
GenAI transformative, and only 18% see revenue impact. When five in six buyers
can't show the CFO a number, the vendor that names its own shelfware controls
the honest number in the room. https://www.hcltech.com/press-releases/hcltech-report-exposes-widening-ai-divide-only-18-enterprises-seeing-revenue-impact

• "Agent-safe" hardened into a category: OneCLI's credential gateway (2.9k
stars — "agents never see the keys"), Common Room gating agent-driven CRM
writes behind identity resolution (duplicate accounts 3% → 0.3%,
vendor-claimed), and — the part that matters — independent corroboration
arriving from outside the vendors themselves. https://github.com/onecli/onecli
