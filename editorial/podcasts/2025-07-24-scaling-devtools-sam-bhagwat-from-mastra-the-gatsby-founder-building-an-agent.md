## What it covers

Bhagwat (previously Gatsby's founder) on how Mastra, a TypeScript AI-agents
framework, grew: docs and changelogs as the primary growth channel, an MCP
server that serves Mastra's own docs to coding agents, a printed
"Principles of Building AI Agents" book handed out at conferences, and an
unusually large Japan user base. No timestamps in the publisher's transcript
beyond `[MM:SS]` per turn.

## Claims worth checking

- [02:12-02:39] ~20% of Mastra's users are in Japan, attributed to dev
  Twitter still functioning as a discovery channel there and viral pickup
  via a Japanese dev-blog platform — Bhagwat's own usage-analytics claim,
  not independently verified. Docs were translated into Japanese as the
  only non-English language specifically because of this.
- [32:47-33:55] Mastra hands out "about 1,500 copies" of its free print book
  per week at meetups/conferences via bulk Amazon orders — a self-reported
  round number, not a verified count, but a concrete, checkable tactic
  (physical book as top-of-funnel, not a gated PDF).
- [09:52-10:10] After one WorkOS-run workshop built a Mastra demo, "five or
  six different groups and 10 different developers" inside WorkOS started
  using Mastra internally — a single anecdote from Bhagwat, not a measured
  conversion.
- [17:34-18:42] Bhagwat's informal cross-batch YC survey: agent startups
  building in TypeScript rose from ~30-35% to ~55-60% "only three months
  later" — his own unverified read of conversations with other founders,
  not a published dataset.

## Why it matters here

Two checkable, still-current tactics for §02 and §06: (1) an MCP server
that exposes a project's own docs to coding agents, installed as a step in
the standard onboarding flow ("make your agent an expert on Mastra") rather
than as an afterthought; (2) a printed, free book as a conference
distribution mechanic, positioned explicitly against a gated PDF. Both are
one founder's account of his own company's growth, with round, unverified
numbers (1,500 books/week, 20% Japan share) — treat as anecdote, not data,
if cited. The changelog-as-marketing argument echoes without adding
independent verification.
