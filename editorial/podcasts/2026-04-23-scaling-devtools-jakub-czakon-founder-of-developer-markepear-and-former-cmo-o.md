## What it covers

Czakon reflects on eight years marketing Neptune.ai (acquired by OpenAI,
undisclosed terms, confirmed on-air) to distill what mattered: picking a hot
market over a big one, keeping website messaging tied to the product you have
today rather than the investor-pitch vision, mining Reddit/HN for the exact
words users use instead of inventing new copy, doubling down on one working
channel (SEO/blog) instead of diversifying early, and front-loading writing
and speech with the conclusion first (reverse pyramid). A late aside covers
paid ads and guerrilla stunts (unauthorized street stickers) as underused
DevTools channels precisely because founders reflexively dismiss them.

## Claims worth checking

- [02:24–04:21] Doubling down on the AI-foundation-model-training vertical
  once that market went "hot and well-funded" is cited as Neptune's biggest
  positioning win. Self-reported, no metrics attached.
- [22:44–25:33] Says Neptune "crushed" SEO/blog growth by choosing not to
  compete head-on with Weights & Biases (well-funded, Valley-embedded,
  researcher-focused) and instead owning MLOps-adjacent search + demand-gen
  content. Anecdotal strategy narrative, not a measured result.
- [09:45–10:35] The "founder-explains-it-differently-than-the-website" test —
  ask a founder to explain the product in one sentence, then compare to the
  live H1/subhead — is offered as a near-universal failure he sees advising
  founders ("eight, nine out of ten" mismatch). Anecdotal, his own consulting
  sample.
- [28:16–29:51] Claims Neptune should have concentrated spend on one working
  channel longer instead of diversifying early — a retrospective regret, not
  a controlled comparison.
