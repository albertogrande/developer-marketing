## What it covers

A follow-up interview where host Jack Bridger, having run his own Technical
Advisory Board (a structured, repeated-interview program with developers who
influence a purchase decision — not just end users), asks Adam Frankl
practical questions about running one: how to keep momentum across a
three-call cycle, how to recruit participants now that cold outreach converts
worse than it used to, and whether to spread interviewing duties across a
team. Frankl's answers are procedural (question design, call cadence,
recruiting via social-media engagement) rather than data-backed.

## Claims worth checking

- [28:12–29:03] Bridger reports cold email now converts near zero for TAB
  recruitment versus a year earlier, and warm intros/events now do the work
  instead; Frankl corroborates ("I'm hearing that from other people too")
  and adds LinkedIn outreach has also degraded, partly from AI-generated spam.
  Both anecdotal, no numbers, two data points not one.
- [44:55–46:03] Frankl's own hypothesis, explicitly unvalidated ("I'm still
  trying to validate this"): long-form video and podcasts are disproportionately
  well-absorbed by LLMs into their training/retrieval versus short-form video,
  making podcast appearances a lever for being cited by AI answers. Stated as
  a working theory, not a measured result — relevant to the standing
  AI-assistants-as-reader thread but not citable as evidence.
- [12:05–12:55] Bridger reports that using AI to summarize/rank TAB interview
  transcripts produced results that "didn't feel very accurate" compared to
  human synthesis, attributing the loss to tone/emotion missing from text
  transcripts. Anecdotal, one team's experience.
- [13:xx] Sourcegraph's original code-search positioning ("the new standard
  developer platform") failed to convert until narrowed to "universal code
  search" — Frankl's own account of a company he worked at, offered as the
  book's example of finding the right two- or three-word framing.

## Why it matters here

Practical detail for `03-devrel-and-community`'s advocacy/research-program
coverage: a structured three-call TAB cadence (discovery → prioritization →
solution-depth test) with concrete interview questions ("what would you wave a
magic wand at," "would duplicating this result be compelling"). The
cold-outreach-degrading claim is worth watching alongside other recruiting-getting-harder
signals before it earns a dated practice entry — right now it's two
practitioners' impressions, not a measured trend.

Weakest part for our purposes: almost every claim here is procedural advice or
one person's anecdote; nothing is a sourced, dated data point the guide could
cite directly.
