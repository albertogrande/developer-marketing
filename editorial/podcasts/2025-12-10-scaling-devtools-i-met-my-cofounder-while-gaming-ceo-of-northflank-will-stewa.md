## What it covers

Will Stewart, cofounder/CEO of Northflank (self-service developer
platform on top of Kubernetes, founded 2019), on building the product for
over two years before any public launch, and on two specific ongoing
communication habits: refusing to publish a public roadmap (verbal
"we're working on it, prioritizing this" instead, so nothing sets a promise
the team can miss), and a monthly changelog detailed enough that customers
have both cited it as their reason for signing up and messaged the company
asking if it was "dying" when a post was skipped.

## Claims worth checking

- Customers have emailed saying they signed up specifically because of how
  detailed Northflank's changelog was — self-reported, anecdotal, no
  count given. [~38:41]
- Users notice and message support when a changelog post is skipped or a
  feature goes a while without a visible update — self-reported, anecdotal.
  [~38:57–39:39]
- Team of 18 engineers maintains feature parity for every capability across
  UI, API, CLI and GitOps, and states the company has "not deprecated a
  single feature" since 2019 — self-reported by the founder, unverified.
  [~26:39, ~28:00]
- Stewart's own retrospective regret: waiting over two years to launch
  publicly cost audience-building time; he says he'd write more content and
  "build in public" earlier if starting again — self-reported opinion, not
  a measured outcome. [~19:47–20:32]

## Why it matters here

Supports a practice: **a detailed, regular changelog can function as an
acquisition channel, not just a release record** — Stewart's account is
that the changelog's absence was noticed as strongly as its presence,
which he reads as evidence users treat it as a trust signal. Anecdotal and
self-reported (no signup or retention numbers attached), so it stays a
practice candidate pending a measured example, not a guide fact. Also a
data point against "launch fast, iterate in public" YC-style advice —
Stewart's counter-view (spend two years hardening the product pre-launch
because "you have to build trust... you can lose trust overnight") is a
position, not evidence either way; worth holding against a future episode
that argues the opposite.
