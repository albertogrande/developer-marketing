## What it covers

A hallway-style conversation recorded at AI Engineer Europe (London), mostly
about running the AI Engineer conference series, then a long detour into
DevTools go-to-market: PLG vs. enterprise sequencing, why Twitter still drives
enterprise deals, and where the DevTools "squeezed middle" is getting crushed
by vibe-coded competition. Closes on AI-generated content and how to signal
you're human.

## Claims worth checking

- [19:14–20:56] swyx's stated consensus: "you want to own PLG because that
  gets you enterprise for free... if you go hard on enterprise, you do not get
  PLG for free" — his own framing/opinion from consulting for Cognition and
  advising coding-tool startups, not sourced data.
- [20:15–20:48] Claim that Twitter specifically (not LinkedIn, not YouTube)
  is still the deciding channel for enterprise coding-tool deals — "my
  developers are asking for this other thing" is what gets an enterprise
  buyer to greenlight a PLG tool. Anecdotal, from his own conversations with
  banks evaluating coding tools.
- [16:45–17:23] His read on the DevTools "squeezed middle": horizontal AI
  frameworks/integration layers ("the React of AI") are getting easily
  reproduced by vibe-coding, while the ends of the barbell — model labs and
  narrow vertical agents — are where value concentrates. Opinion, stated
  plainly as such, not a study.
- [1:19–1:44] AI Engineer's own growth mechanic: a JSConf-style regional
  franchise model (assets, marketing, YouTube channel, and speaker/sponsor
  support handed to trusted local organizers) — Melbourne, Singapore, Miami,
  Paris, and an unannounced Shanghai edition this year. Stated as fact about
  their own operation, not third-party verified, but a concrete, copyable
  community-scaling mechanic.
- [15:12] "A year-old company... bought by DoorDash, $450,000,000 for seven
  employees" (RL-environments startup) — named in passing, no company name
  given on the episode; unverifiable from this transcript alone, do not cite
  without independent confirmation.
