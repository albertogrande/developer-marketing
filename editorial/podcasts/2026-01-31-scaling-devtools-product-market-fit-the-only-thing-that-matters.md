## What it covers

Another solo Jack Bridger episode: he walks through Marc Andreessen's 2007
"only thing that matters" essay (market beats team and product), plays a clip
of a Michael Seibel/Craig Cannon interview on how founders misdiagnose product-market
fit, and closes with his own commentary — including on LayerCode, his own
company — plus a friend's argument that AI is raising the bar for what counts
as product-market fit in some categories.

## Claims worth checking

- [24:10–25:01] Argument (from an article a friend sent Jack, not verified
  independently in the episode) that AI is raising customer expectations fast
  enough in some categories to collapse existing product-market fit — Chegg's
  homework-help product cited as an example of a market AI reportedly
  hollowed out. Secondhand and anecdotal; no data cited.
- [23:09–23:47] Jack's own read on Supabase: he argues its product didn't
  change but its positioning did — going from unnoticed to "open source
  Firebase" reframed lock-in avoidance as the thing developers suddenly cared
  about. His own analysis, not sourced to Supabase or any interview.
- [12:47–17:52] Michael Seibel's definition (from a separate, older YC
  interview, replayed here): product-market fit is explosive, profitable
  usage growth — not "I built what customers say they want." He states most
  founders, including most YC founders, never reach it. Secondhand clip,
  no numbers given for the "most founders" claim.

## Quotes

> "It's not like when I go from zero customers to one customer, have not hit

> "Founders often hold too tightly onto solutions and too loosely onto

## Why it matters here

General startup strategy content, not developer-marketing-specific — most of
the episode is Jack narrating two other people's public essays/interviews
(Andreessen 2007, Seibel/Cannon) rather than original reporting. The Supabase
positioning read is Jack's own opinion, not sourced from Supabase, so it is
not citable as a fact — at most an illustrative anecdote for
`01-positioning-for-developers` on how a narrative reframe (not a product
change) can shift adoption. The AI-collapses-PMF claim is worth watching for a
sourced version elsewhere; as presented here it is unverified.
