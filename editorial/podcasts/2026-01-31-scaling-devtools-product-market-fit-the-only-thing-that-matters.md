## What it covers

A solo, no-guest episode: host Jack Bridger reads through Marc Andreessen's
2007 "the only thing that matters" essay (team vs. product vs. market as the
predictor of startup success), plays a clip of a Michael Seibel/Craig Cannon
interview on how founders self-deceive about having found product-market fit,
and closes with a friend's note on how AI raises the bar a market demands
before a product satisfies it.

## Claims worth checking

- [03:44–05:23] Andreessen's core claim, quoted at length: in a great market
  the market "pulls product out of the startup" and a weak team can still
  succeed, while a great team and product in a bad market still fails.
  Restated theory from a 2007 essay, not a new data point — timeless framework,
  not a dated fact.
- [16:00] Seibel's claim that "the vast majority of YC founders even never find
  product market fit" is his stated experience running YC, not a cited figure.
- [24:10–25:01] The friend's argument (paraphrased, not quoted directly) that
  AI raises customer expectations fast enough to collapse an existing
  product-market fit — Chegg's homework-help business is given as the
  example. Anecdotal illustration, no measurement of the claimed mechanism.

## Why it matters here

Restates two already-canonical startup essays (Andreessen 2007, Seibel's PMF
interview) with no new dated evidence — exactly the "model already knows
this" case `editorial/MEMORY.md`'s practices bar warns against. The one
semi-fresh thread, AI collapsing product-market fit faster than products can
adapt, is asserted secondhand from an unnamed article and not evidenced here;
worth re-reading if that argument surfaces again with a named source. Nothing
in this episode is developer-marketing-specific — it's general SaaS startup
theory, cited via a devtools podcast.
