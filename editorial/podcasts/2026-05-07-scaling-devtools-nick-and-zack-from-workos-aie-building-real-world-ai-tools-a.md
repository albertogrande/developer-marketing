## What it covers

An in-person AI Engineer Europe conversation with two WorkOS engineers who run
the company's conference workshops, mostly a personal-workflow chat about
using Claude/agent "skills" for daily engineering work, plus a shorter segment
on what makes a devtools conference workshop land with an audience.

## Claims worth checking

- [16:39–17:35, 23:15–24:02] Their stated workshop philosophy: build the
  session entirely around teaching real technique (this round: Claude
  "skills"), with a single logo slide and one mention of hiring — no
  product-pitch framing — because, in their account, that is what got them
  invited back a year after their first workshop. Self-reported, anecdotal,
  no audience data.
- [17:56–18:45] Second-time lesson: they explicitly designed the session for
  the eventual YouTube recording as well as the room, including built-in
  "work for twenty minutes" blocks and open Q&A stretches, after the first
  workshop's un-miked audience moments (including an unplanned Sam Bhagwat
  cameo) didn't translate to video. Anecdotal, single event.
- [18:45–19:22, 20:03–20:39] Practical workshop mechanics reported as
  effective: publishing the GitHub repo in advance so attendees can start
  early, and a "checkpoint" branch structure so anyone who falls behind or
  wants to skip ahead can jump to any stage. Anecdotal.

## Why it matters here

Thin, single-source evidence for `03-devrel-and-community` on workshop design
(content-first framing, design-for-video, checkpoint/branch structure) — all
one team's anecdotal account of two workshops, not measured against
attendance, follow-on, or lead data. The bulk of the episode is AI-agent
personal-workflow chat (biometric-gated Claude nudges, "harness engineering,"
OpenClaw hacking) that is not developer-marketing evidence; not treated as
such here.
