## What it covers

Not a standard interview — the host reads through Lee Robinson's published
writing (leerob.com) and clips from other podcasts shortly after Robinson
left Vercel (where he is credited with taking it from $0 to $200M ARR) to
lead developer education at Cursor. Organized around three of Robinson's own
frameworks: what makes developer docs work, what community actually is, and
what "great developer marketing" means as distinct from marketing in general.

## Claims worth checking

- [01:49–02:24] Robinson's docs framework, as quoted: nine concrete rules
  (code examples that copy-paste-run, optimize for skimming, avoid jargon,
  simple-first then reveal complexity, document known workarounds even for
  product gaps, AI-assisted review passes for spelling/brevity, embed docs
  in-editor for in-context feedback, automated broken-link checks). A named
  practitioner's checklist, not independently tested, but specific enough to
  audit against.
- [08:08–10:54] Community framed explicitly as reciprocal, not transactional:
  "it's not a collection of developers you're trying to sell your product
  to... they share bugs, you fix them; they submit pull requests, you merge
  and encourage them." Robinson's stated principle — most teams reportedly
  skip the "tell them" step after fixing a reported issue, relying on a
  broadcast announcement instead of circling back to the specific person.
  Self-reported observation, not measured.
- [15:18–16:55] "Your product is your best marketing": Robinson's claim that
  developers are drawn to learning *how* a well-built product was built, and
  that explaining implementation choices is itself the marketing. Framed as
  a belief about developer psychology, not tested.
- [17:14–20:40] On trust: "developers won't try your product the first time
  they hear about it... they need to hear positive feedback several times."
  No cited study — a practitioner's rule of thumb repeated frequently enough
  in DevRel circles to be treated as received wisdom, worth flagging as
  unsourced if it were ever promoted into a guide claim.
- [29:46–30:53] A named tactic from Vercel (via a clip of Gonto, ex-Vercel):
  a "product advocates" team hired from Apple Genius Bar / Best Buy tech
  support / CS bootcamps — technical enough to help, cheap enough to run as
  SDRs — who reached out based on behavioral blocked-signals (clicked into a
  dashboard section, went to docs, came back, did nothing) rather than lead
  score. Self-reported, no conversion numbers given, but a specific,
  copyable trigger-based outreach design.
