## What it covers

Kim Maida walks through the five-stage DevRel flywheel she first published in
2022 — inspire → educate → activate → empower → champion — and argues it has
held up unchanged since. The back half turns to measurement: why "awareness" as
DevRel's single metric is the problem, and why she expects to need metrics at
Keycard even as a team of one. A third strand covers writing docs for agents as
a first-party audience rather than a side effect.

## Claims worth checking

- [26:30–28:10] The flywheel was designed specifically to give executives a way
  to describe DevRel's financial value in the vocabulary the rest of the org
  already uses (MQLs/SQLs) — its origin is an attribution-argument tool, not a
  content-planning tool. Reported as her own intent; no data attached.
- [32:35] She names asking an LLM whether a product fits a use case as a normal
  step in the evaluate stage now, alongside reading the docs and the API
  reference. Anecdotal, stated as her own current behaviour.
- [33:25–33:59] Her claim that a bad first experience loses the developer
  permanently — "you don't necessarily get those people back" — is asserted from
  experience, not measured. Worth testing against activation data before the
  guide leans on it.
- [13:21] Keycard treats agent-accessible docs as a first-party concern. One
  company's practice, not evidence of a trend.
- [37:02] Social proof described as decisive at the top of the funnel, in the
  plain sense that a public negative verdict stops the trial before it starts.

## Why it matters here

Direct corroboration for the guide's measurement section: a named practitioner
saying the awareness-only metric is what makes DevRel indefensible in budget
conversations, which is the same argument `08-measurement-and-metrics` makes.
The flywheel is a candidate framework to cite — it is public, dated 2022, and
its author says it has not needed revision, which is a stronger claim than most
DevRel frameworks can make.

Weakest part for our purposes: every measurement claim is experiential. Nothing
here is a data point, so it belongs in the guide as an attributed practitioner
view, never as evidence.
