## What it covers

Mostly an engineering-philosophy conversation (AI's effect on code review,
observability as "validation" for AI-generated code, durable vs. disposable
code) with a closing stretch on how Majors has used personal-brand writing and
speaking to build Honeycomb, and why she's writing a second edition of
*Observability Engineering*.

## Claims worth checking

- [28:07–29:20] States plainly that Honeycomb's early go-to-market was
  founder-led conference speaking ("any place that invites Charity to speak,
  she says yes") while an early engineering hire (Emily Nakashima) ran
  engineering day-to-day — an explicit division she says let her spend years
  "constantly on Twitter" and traveling. Self-reported, single company,
  eight-plus years old as a strategy.
- [29:30–30:06] Tactic, stated as deliberate: publish something externally
  (blog, talk) rather than only internally, because employees pay more
  attention to a company's own claims when they see them validated outside
  the building first. Anecdotal, no measurement offered.
- [37:21–38:30] On whether a devtools founder should write a book:
  recommends it specifically *if* it can land with a recognized technical
  publisher (she names O'Reilly) for the credibility/branding effect;
  says a blog might have served the same content better in retrospect,
  given how much of the last year the second-edition rewrite consumed.
  Single-founder opinion, explicitly hedged by her own regret.
