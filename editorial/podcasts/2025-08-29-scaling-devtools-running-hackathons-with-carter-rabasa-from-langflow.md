## What it covers

A DevRel lead's operating framework for developer hackathons: when they help
versus waste money (a "Goldilocks" maturity window), what they actually cost,
and how they fit inside a broader field-marketing / community strategy.

## Claims worth checking

- [05:58–08:32] Hackathons are the wrong tool if the request originates from
  marketing alone, and are actively harmful for a product that is too early
  (buggy, thin docs) — "you will spend time and money teaching developers
  that your platform is not reliable." Self-reported operating rule, not
  measured, but stated as a hard anti-pattern rather than a soft preference.
- [23:44–25:24] Frames product maturity as a bell curve: too early wastes the
  event, too late (a fully-adopted product like S3) has little left to prove;
  the useful window is the middle ~80%. Practitioner's heuristic, unverified
  against outcome data.
- [32:11–33:31] Langflow's own budget range for running a hackathon end-to-end:
  $5,000–$25,000, depending on whether venue/catering are sponsored (Cloudflare
  hosted their San Francisco event for free). Self-reported, company-specific
  figures, not audited.
- [09:12–11:32] The core claimed payoff isn't awareness but a product-feedback
  loop: engineers sitting shoulder-to-shoulder with the tool for a full
  24–48 hours surface friction that a paid one-hour user-research session
  can't, because participants are motivated and reluctant to ask for help
  before docs fail them. Anecdotal, no comparison data cited.
- [1:29:04–1:32:05] Langflow ran three hackathons in ~4–5 months (San
  Francisco, New York, London — the most recent, ~130–135 attendees against
  a ~150 cap) and now runs on a repeat cadence in a fixed set of cities
  chosen for AI-engineering density, not audience size alone.
- [1:44:11–1:44:27] Names a specific anti-pattern in event marketing broadly:
  some AI conferences list speakers who never agreed to speak — verified
  directly with founders he knows personally who said they'd never heard of
  the event. Anecdotal, second-hand for the reader, but a concrete named
  practice (do due diligence on a sponsorship target's speaker list) rather
  than a vague warning.

## Why it matters here

On-topic for `03-devrel-and-community`, the guide's thinnest section (per
the evergreen-shelf note in MEMORY.md on the champions-program ledger — this
episode is adjacent, event budgeting rather than advocate programs, but the
same "what does this actually cost and return" gap applies). Everything here
is one DevRel lead's self-reported process and budget from a single company;
useful as a named, dated data point (the $5–25K range, the 3-events-in-5-
months cadence) but not evidence that hackathons work in general — no
conversion or pipeline numbers are given, only the qualitative
product-feedback and awareness case.
