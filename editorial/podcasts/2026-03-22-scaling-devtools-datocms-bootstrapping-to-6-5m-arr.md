## What it covers

Stefano and Matteo trace DatoCMS from a one-person side project inside their
web agency to a 13-person, fully-remote company doing €6.5M ARR and 3B API
calls a month — bootstrapped, no funding until 2019. The conversation covers
why they never raised, how being cheap and small became a durable
positioning choice against better-funded headless-CMS competitors, their
Shape Up-derived six-week cycle for scoping features, and how they built
trust with customers (fast personal support, proactive refunds, direct
founder access).

## Claims worth checking

- [03:17–03:46] ~200 agencies use DatoCMS regularly and the company does
  "almost no marketing" — growth is word-of-mouth. Self-reported, no
  independent corroboration.
- [04:06–05:22] They credit 2020's rapid growth (ramen-profitable to ~€1M
  revenue in a year, on 3 people) largely to riding the Jamstack/Netlify/Gatsby
  wave and being showcased in Netlify's own integration marketing —
  co-marketing with a platform partner as a growth channel. Their own account,
  not measured against a counterfactual.
- [39:44] Two support people currently serve 1,500–2,000 customers. Self-reported
  headcount ratio, not audited.
- [31:16–31:26] Their prior Heroku bill was ~$150–200K/year; migrating to
  self-managed AWS/Kubernetes cost roughly half that for significantly more
  performance (databases ~half the size, web servers ~3x faster). Self-reported,
  no third-party benchmark.

## Why it matters here

A concrete counter-example to the venture-funded devtool playbook the guide
mostly assumes: cost as a deliberate positioning axis (§01), not just a
constraint, paired with founder-level support responsiveness as a trust
signal. The refund-as-goodwill and "jump on a call" habits are testable,
low-cost practices a smaller devtool could copy directly.

Weakest part for our purposes: every growth and support claim is
self-reported by the founders, with no usage data, churn figures, or
independent verification offered.
