## What it covers

An events-organizer interview: Carey runs AI Demo Days (5–10 minute rapid-fire
startup demo nights, no long-format talks) and lays out, with real numbers,
what different event formats cost and who actually gets value from them —
evening demo nights, hackathons, and conference-scale events.

## Claims worth checking

- [~03:40] An evening demo-night venue in London runs roughly £2,000–£4,000
  (~50 attendees). Founder-stated, one operator's cost data, not audited.
- [~14:00] A single-day hackathon venue alone runs ~£20,000; a weekend
  hackathon roughly doubles that. His argument: remote hackathons capture
  none of the in-person networking value, so the cost/benefit collapses
  entirely for a company running one to "get brand recognition."
- [~09:00–13:00] His mechanism for who actually funds these events: VCs
  sponsor because it's good for deal flow, portfolio-company hiring, and
  image — not because the organizing company profits directly. Every AI Demo
  Days event since the first has been VC- or company-sponsored, never
  attendee-charged.
- [~32:00] His claim, stated plainly as opinion: a company-run event only
  works if it doesn't look like a company event — the best events "have no
  one's stamp." He contrasts Anthropic's YouTube technical lecture series
  (which he says works because "people want to be close to them") against a
  company trying to run a community demo night under its own brand (which he
  says changes who shows up and kills the vibe). Anecdotal judgment, not
  measured.

## Why it matters here

Useful, concrete cost data for §03 (DevRel and community), which the guide
coverage index flags as its thinnest section — this is the kind of ledger the
"champions program" evergreen-shelf item could eventually cite alongside. The
hackathon-ROI argument is one practitioner's strong opinion, backed by
specific cost breakdowns but no controlled comparison (he never measures an
actual hire, signup, or revenue outcome against the spend) — treat the cost
numbers as data and the ROI verdict as a position.
