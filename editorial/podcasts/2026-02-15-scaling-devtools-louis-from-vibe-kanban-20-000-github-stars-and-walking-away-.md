## What it covers

Louis traces Vibe Kanban's path from a YC-community side project to ~20,000
GitHub stars, and contrasts it with his team's two earlier companies (an
embedding-based code search engine, then COBOL-modernization services) that
looked similar on paper but never built a real community. The second half
covers how the team decides what to build against fast-moving AI labs, how
AI-authored pull requests changed the bar for a "serious" community
contribution, and why the team walked away from six-figure enterprise/bank
deals to refocus on developers.

## Claims worth checking

- [10:42–11:13] Vibe Kanban's Discord has "thousands" of members versus ~100
  for the team's earlier open-source project, despite the earlier one having
  more GitHub stars and downloads — his read: stars measure visibility, not
  community. Self-reported, no independent numbers.
- [31:04–31:38] Distinguishes "source available" (code happens to be public,
  no one opens PRs) from real open source (breadth of channels — feedback
  box, GitHub issues/PRs/discussions, Discord, direct email, Reddit — plus
  visibly responding). Anecdotal framework, not measured, but a concrete
  practitioner distinction worth testing.
- [28:03–28:51] AI-generated pull requests raised the bar for what counts as
  a serious bug report: "more than half of the things we ship from the
  community" now arrive as an AI-drafted PR rather than a Discord complaint,
  because the reporter has already tested that their fix works. Self-reported,
  one company's experience.
- [00:33–01:16] Internal build/no-build heuristic: don't build what the
  frontier lab (their example: the Claude Code team) is likely to ship within
  three months, but anything on a ~12-month horizon is fair game since "if you
  take a twelve month view, almost everything gets built by this team" and
  waiting on that basis means building nothing. Anecdotal, framed as internal
  rule of thumb, not evidence of a general strategy.
- [33:04–35:11] Reason for abandoning six-figure bank/enterprise deals: 18–24
  month sales cycles eating limited runway, plus deal risk from the buyer's
  internal chaos (one signed customer's champion was fired and the deal
  cancelled overnight, unrelated to product or competitors). Anecdotal,
  single-company account.

## Why it matters here

Concrete, testable material for `03-devrel-and-community`: the
stars-vs-community distinction and the channel-breadth practice are both
specific enough to become a practice unit ("when open-sourcing a tool, do
X because Y"), and the AI-PR-as-complaint-bar claim is a dated (2026) shift
worth flagging if a second practitioner corroborates it. Weakest part: every
claim here is one founder's retrospective on his own company, with no
adoption numbers or outside verification — treat as an attributed practitioner
view, not a data point, if it reaches the guide.
