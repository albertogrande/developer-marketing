## What it covers

Kyle Cheung, cofounder of Greybeam (routes Snowflake BI queries to DuckDB,
claims 71–95% cost cuts, no migration), on the company's deliberately
absurdist LinkedIn/video marketing: a running "toilet flush" gag (big flush
= a heavy Snowflake query, small flush = a cheap DuckDB one), stunt videos
(rolling out from under a desk, a duck-and-banana microphone at conferences),
and low-cost, high-intentionality swag and customer-story production (flying
to interview a customer on his private plane rather than filming a standard
case study).

## Claims worth checking

- "86% average Snowflake cost savings" — self-reported, Greybeam's own
  figure; the H1 messaging was updated from "70%" to "86%" as the claimed
  average changed. [~19:00]
- Typical customer Snowflake spend bands (self-reported, anecdotal, from
  Cheung's own sales conversations): $50–100K for smaller teams, ~$300K±200K
  for mature companies, $1M–25M for late-startup/early-enterprise. [~08:30]
- Hero messaging was iterated from a broad claim ("route your Snowflake
  queries to DuckDB and save 70%") to a narrower, workload-specific one
  ("real Snowflake BI queries to DuckDB, save 86%") after broad messaging
  pulled in demo bookings that didn't have the specific pain Greybeam
  solves — self-reported, anecdotal. [~18:15]

## Why it matters here

An anecdotal, single-founder data point for the **earned distribution /
"different beats better"** thread (`editorial/MEMORY.md`) — irreverent,
zero-budget content (LinkedIn stunt videos, a running visual gag tied
directly to the product's mechanism) as a pattern-interruption tactic for
an early-stage, unfunded devtool, alongside RevenueCat and PostHog as
named comparables Cheung cites. Not evidence that the tactic drives revenue
or retention — Cheung says explicitly he doesn't know where the "too silly"
wall is and hasn't hit it yet. No claim here belongs in the guide as fact;
it is one founder's position on brand differentiation, not measured data.
