## What it covers

Recorded live at LeadDev's LDX3 London (2,500+ engineering leaders). Fletcher
shares LeadDev's own research program — 150 buyer interviews since January
plus whole-audience quant surveys — on what engineering leaders are buying
and how they react to AI-first vendor messaging. No timestamps in the
publisher's transcript (plain speaker labels only), so positions below are
described by order, not time.

## Claims worth checking

- [early] 52% of LeadDev's audience report an intent to buy AI coding tools
  in the next 12 months; ~20% report buying CI/CD, monitoring/observability,
  and QA/testing tools in the same window — self-reported by LeadDev from its
  own survey panel, not independently verified, and the panel is LeadDev's
  own (self-selected) audience.
- [early] More senior audience members (VPs, directors) report buying
  downstream tooling (monitoring/observability) at nearly the same rate as AI
  coding tools — Fletcher's read of the same survey, framed as "where the
  pain is now that code generation is automated." Self-reported, one
  research org's segmentation.
- [mid] "Only a little over half" of LeadDev's audience holds a positive view
  of AI, and strong AI-first vendor messaging measurably pushes skeptical
  engineering buyers away — self-reported qualitative finding, no published
  number beyond "a little over half."
- [late] The share of LeadDev's audience reporting they are increasing
  devtools spend year over year rose from 27% two years ago to 36% this year
  (a two-year change, not a single-year one; Fletcher hedges both numbers with
  "I think") — self-reported, LeadDev's own panel; directionally useful but
  not a market-wide figure.

## Why it matters here

Fletcher's advice lines up with this site's existing proof-over-adjectives
position — lead with the specific pain point and a checkable example, not
AI-first headline claims — but here it comes with LeadDev's own buyer-research
numbers behind it, which the guide currently doesn't cite. It's still one
research org's self-run panel, not a third-party study, and LeadDev sells
sponsorships to the same devtools vendors it's advising, so treat the
survey numbers as directionally credible rather than independently audited.
The specific, checkable claim worth carrying into a practice: AI-first
messaging measurably alienates a skeptical majority of engineering buyers,
and the counter-move is a specifics-led talk (their example: Honeycomb's
Liz Fong-Jones on AI adoption, "not big headline figures, but the specifics
of how they got there").
