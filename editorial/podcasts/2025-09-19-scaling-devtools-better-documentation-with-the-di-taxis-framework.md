## What it covers

A solo, working-notes episode: Jack applies the Diátaxis documentation
framework (tutorials / how-to guides / reference / explanation — a 2x2 of
action-vs-cognition and skill-acquisition-vs-application) to his own product's
docs live on air, walks through how Sequin and Logd structured their docs
around it, and reads out mixed Hacker News reactions.

## Claims worth checking

- [~08:10] Sequin's founder is quoted (via a since-unlinked HN comment cited
  in the episode, not independently re-verified here) saying they applied
  Diátaxis to their docs and found it easier to know where new content
  belongs — self-reported, single company, no metric attached ("felt so
  nice," not a measured outcome).
- [~19:00–21:00] HN pushback, read aloud and worth keeping distinct from the
  framework's proponents: technical writers cited "content reuse" (same
  content needed across docs/UI/mobile) as a case Diátaxis' four fixed
  buckets don't handle well, and one commenter reported being told their docs
  were "not acceptable" for mixing modalities during a sprint review — cited
  as evidence the framework can be applied too rigidly.
- No adoption numbers, no before/after traffic or support-ticket data from
  any company mentioned — every account in the episode is anecdotal, "it
  helped me organize my thinking," not measured impact.

## Quotes

## Why it matters here

Directly on-topic for §02 (docs as front door) and a clean example of a
practitioner narrating their own docs-restructuring process in real time,
which is rarer than after-the-fact case studies. Nothing here is a measured
result — treat the framework's value as argued, not demonstrated — but Sequin
and Logd are named, linkable, currently-restructured docs sites that could be
checked directly as example-candidates if someone wants to verify the
before/after claim rather than take the quoted account on faith.
