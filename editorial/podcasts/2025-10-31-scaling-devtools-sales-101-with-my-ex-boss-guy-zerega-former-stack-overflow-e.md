## What it covers

A sales-leadership retrospective from Stack Overflow's former EVP of Revenue,
built around "know your audience" applied specifically to developers: they
prefer the written word to a phone call, they're trained to spot errors (so a
sloppy outreach email loses credibility instantly), and outreach quality
compounds trust. The second half is founder-stage advice — building an ideal
candidate profile for a first sales hire, why ex-big-company salespeople
often struggle at startups with no collateral to lean on, and why he
recommends hiring three salespeople rather than two so a bad result can be
attributed to the person, not the product.

## Claims worth checking

- [26:11–26:42] His account of Stack Overflow discovering that adding a
  salesperson to a self-serve job-listing purchase roughly doubled what a
  self-serve customer was willing to spend. Self-reported from memory, no
  figures cited beyond "dramatic."
- [22:25–23:09] Rule of thumb stated as SaaS-sales convention rather than his
  own data: roughly 20% of salespeople generate 80% of revenue, and once
  fewer than ~80% of a sales team is hitting quota, that's a signal to stop
  hiring and diagnose rather than keep scaling.
- [28:40–29:16] His read on Vercel's early sales hires as the reason Vercel
  built developer-community trust — explicitly labelled "my interpretation of
  what they did there", not a Vercel-sourced account. Note the specifics
  (hiring technical, Apple-support-type people) come from the host's question
  at [27:56], citing an article by a former Vercel CMO, not from Zerega.
- [32:37–33:32] Claim that colleague referral/word-of-mouth has become more
  decisive in buying decisions recently ("more today than a year ago") than
  self-directed online research, attributed to information overload.
  Anecdotal, undated, no data behind the "more than a year ago" comparison.
- [13:13] "Always be closing" reframed as "always be getting
  commitment on next steps" rather than a one-call close — his own
  reinterpretation of a well-known sales maxim, not a new finding.

## Why it matters here

Relevant to `03-devrel-and-community` and the hiring/PMM sections mostly as
attributed practitioner opinion on selling to developers specifically —
written-word preference, error-sensitivity, trust-building — none of it
measured. This is a career-retrospective interview, not a case study with
numbers; treat everything here as one experienced operator's judgment, not
evidence a claim could cite.
