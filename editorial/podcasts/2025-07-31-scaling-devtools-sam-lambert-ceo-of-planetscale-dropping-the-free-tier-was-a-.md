## What it covers

Recorded shortly after PlanetScale's Postgres launch. Lambert explains why
PlanetScale killed its free tier, how the company decides what not to
support, and its house style for technical content and launches. No
timestamps in the publisher's VTT beyond `[MM:SS]` markers on each turn.

## Claims worth checking

- [15:02] PlanetScale's entry-level paid tier starts at $39/month; the
  company deliberately does not support hobby/free usage — Lambert's own
  stated positioning, not third-party verified, but a plain fact about
  PlanetScale's current pricing page.
- [17:05] Lambert's generalized claim: users who convert to paid do so within
  the first one to two weeks of a free trial, and users still on a free tier
  after three months "probably never will" convert — self-reported executive
  pattern-matching from PlanetScale's own historical free-tier data, not a
  published number or methodology.
- [16:06] Some LLM assistants continued recommending PlanetScale as having a
  free tier well after it was discontinued (unspecified date), producing
  user complaints — Lambert's anecdote, not independently measured, but a
  concrete example of stale AI-answer-engine claims about a vendor's own
  pricing (relevant to the aeo beat's "answer engines get facts wrong"
  pattern already published from Profound's research, 2026-08-20).
- [76:00-ish, after killing the free tier] Lambert frames free-tier spend as
  a marketing-spend line item most companies don't honestly report against
  conversion — an opinion/framework, not a measured claim.

## Why it matters here

Two separate, checkable practices here. First: PlanetScale launches with a
named customer already live on the feature rather than a solo announcement
— the same proof-over-adjectives mechanic this site already tracks, applied
to launches specifically rather than deprecations or benchmarks. Second: a
concrete counter-example to "always keep a free tier" — Lambert names the
tradeoff explicitly (marginal infra cost isn't free for a database vendor
the way it is for a pure SaaS product) rather than treating "free tier
drives growth" as universal advice. Both are one CEO's self-reported
read of his own company's data, not an independent study — no churn/
conversion numbers are published, only Lambert's characterization of them.
