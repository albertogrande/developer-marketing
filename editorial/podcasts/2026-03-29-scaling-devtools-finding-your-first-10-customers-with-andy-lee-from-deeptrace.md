## What it covers

Andy Lee (technical founder, no go-to-market hires) walks through how his
four-person engineering team landed DeepTrace's first ~10 customers on pure
outbound volume: ~250 LinkedIn connection requests per person per week
(~1,000/week team-wide) for two months during YC, hand-written persona-based
templates (not LLM-generated), and calls opened with personal story before
any discovery questions. Also covers champion-selection heuristics (favors
people with prior startup experience and a visible "rebel" online presence
over seniority alone), an internal Twitter/X-scraping tool built with Claude
Code to find engineers publicly complaining about incidents/on-call pain,
and an early experiment generating ad-copy SVGs with Claude Code to
mass-test paid-ad variations.

## Claims worth checking

- [15:03–15:34] Reports ~40% LinkedIn connection-accept rate and roughly
  half-to-75% of those responding, at ~250 messages/person/week. Self-reported,
  single company, no independent verification — a specific, falsifiable
  benchmark number if the weekly editor wants to cite it, but attribute it.
  Explicitly caveated by Lee himself as context-dependent (compliance-to-startup
  vs. enterprise sales differ).
- [16:11–16:43] Claims younger/more-junior people converted better than
  seniority-matched targets because they were "more AI-native" — his own
  post-hoc read of call patterns, not a controlled test.
- [09:57–10:17] States templates were hand-written, explicitly not
  LLM-generated, and that this outperformed hyper-personalization in their
  experience — anecdotal, one company, contradicts "a different school of
  thought" he acknowledges exists.
- Company/product framing (DeepTrace: AI reliability platform, ex-Tesla/
  SpaceX founders, early customers include Opendoor and Rain) is
  self-reported on the episode; not independently verified here.

## Why it matters here

A concrete, testable early-stage outbound playbook from a technical founder
with no sales hires — fits the practice bar (specific numbers: volume,
accept rate, response rate) better than most founder-story episodes, though
every number is single-company and self-reported. The Claude-Code-for-sales-
tooling thread (X-scraping for pain-point signals, SVG ad-copy generation)
is a small, concrete example of engineers building their own GTM tooling
rather than buying it — worth watching for a second, independent example
before it's citable as a pattern.
