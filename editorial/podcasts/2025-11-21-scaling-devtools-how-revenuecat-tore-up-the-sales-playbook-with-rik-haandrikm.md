## What it covers

Rik Haandrikman (VP of Growth, RevenueCat — mobile subscription
infrastructure) describes a sales org deliberately not compensated on
signed-contract value: reps are credited for helping a prospect evaluate
and buy however they want (credit card swipe or contract), which he says
removes the incentive to push self-serve developers into unwanted
contracts. The second half covers "compelling events" — platform-forced
moments (Google Play Billing Library major-version deprecations) that
RevenueCat builds demand-gen around, including running free migration help
for non-customers to build trust ahead of the next compelling event — and
a company-wide, quirky-booth conference-attendance policy (support,
finance, legal all staff booths) as a trust-building tactic distinct from
lead generation.

## Claims worth checking

- Comp-structure natural experiment: RevenueCat once compensated reps on
  contract *count* rather than value; self-serve credit-card conversions
  fell, signed contracts rose, and total revenue was roughly flat —
  self-reported, an internal before/after RevenueCat says it reversed
  quickly, no external verification. [~09:15]
- "Closing in on 50% of new subscription apps worldwide launch with
  RevenueCat," and RevenueCat is in ~30% of subscription apps but handles
  ~20% of subscription-app revenue in the app stores — all self-reported
  company figures, no source cited beyond "I was looking at the numbers
  today." [~20:15]
- Free, non-sales technical support for Google Play Billing Library v4→v5
  migration (a major breaking change) offered to non-customers, credited
  with converting "a bunch" of them at the next compelling event —
  self-reported, anecdotal, no count given. [~18:19]
- Bitrise's Tokyo meetup strategy: an early meetup contact went on to found
  "one of Japan's biggest startup successes," making that company Bitrise's
  #2 market "in the span of months" — single anecdotal case, explicitly
  flagged by Haandrikman as partly luck. [~27:29]

## Why it matters here

A named-company, on-record account (not anonymous survey data) for the
**DevRel measurement / influence-not-attribution** thread
(`editorial/MEMORY.md`): RevenueCat structurally removes contract-value
comp to keep self-serve PLG and sales-assisted paths both healthy, and
treats compelling-event-triggered support (even for non-customers) as the
demand-gen unit instead of MQLs. Also a data point for the champions/events
evergreen-shelf candidate — company-wide conference staffing as a trust
tactic, explicitly not measured against booth cost. All growth and revenue
figures are self-reported by RevenueCat with no external source; usable as
a named practice ("align rep comp with self-serve, not against it"), not
as verified market data.
