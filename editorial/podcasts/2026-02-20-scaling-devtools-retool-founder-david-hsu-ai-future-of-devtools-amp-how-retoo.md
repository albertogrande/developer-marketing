## What it covers

David Hsu covers two threads: Retool's shift from a pure developer tool
toward "tomorrow's developers" (semi-technical, SQL-capable non-engineers)
and toward agents/automations as its fastest-growing revenue slice, and the
origin story of how Retool found its first customers — including a failed
cold-outreach experiment posing as a FileMaker developer in a LinkedIn
group, and the YC batch that produced their first two paying customers.

## Claims worth checking

- [22:01–22:20] Retool says the share of non-developers building on the
  platform rose from ~30% two years ago to ~60% today, and that this
  developer-to-non-developer shift predates and is independent of LLMs, only
  accelerated by them. Self-reported internal usage stat, no methodology
  given.
- [31:09–32:34] The FileMaker-developer cold outreach (infiltrating a
  LinkedIn group, messaging ~100–200 people) produced 4 replies and 3
  explicit rejections — offered as a cautionary example of a positioning
  hypothesis that was "dead wrong," not a success case.
- [37:xx] Retool's early ICP-narrowing heuristic — "pragmatic engineer" +
  "operationally heavy, fast-scaling company" — is presented as the
  intersection that worked, arrived at around the fourth tested hypothesis.
  Self-reported, not independently verified.
- [45:19–46:49] Hsu's claim that Tableau has built a durable moat AI can't
  easily route around ("I'd rather just buy Tableau"), contrasted with Cursor
  having "not that substantial" a moat despite being a viable business — his
  own competitive analysis, asserted without data.

## Why it matters here

Bears on §01 (positioning) and §04 (activation): a specific, falsifiable
account of an ICP-narrowing process (four tested hypotheses, three that
failed) rather than the more common survivorship-bias story that only shows
the winning segment. The FileMaker-outreach failure is a rare on-record
example of a positioning bet that didn't work, worth citing precisely
because most founders don't share those.

Weakest part for our purposes: the 30%→60% non-developer adoption figure is
the episode's one quantitative claim and it's unaudited internal telemetry
from the company being profiled — treat as a directional, not a verified,
number.
