## What it covers

Ahmad argues seed-stage devtools don't need large rounds ($2–4M gets ~2
years' runway in the Bay Area) and that overhiring, not competition, kills
startups ("death by suicide"). The bulk of the episode is his tactical
account of finding PMF for Corgea after an early pivot: rejecting design
partnerships in favor of pushing prospects to a hard yes/no, tracking how
many first-meetings convert to a pilot as the real PMF signal, and mining
customer complaints ("50 reasons people don't like their AppSec tools") to
find where incumbent tools were weakest.

## Claims worth checking

- [02:20–03:00] His own $2.5M "you don't need more" claim, posted on
  LinkedIn, drew pushback rather than going viral — stated by him as
  friction, not measured engagement data.
- [21:59–22:07] His rule of thumb: 60–80% of second meetings should convert
  to a pilot/POC once a product is working — offered as his own heuristic,
  not benchmarked against other founders' funnels.
- [11:01–11:18] Corgea pivoted four months into YC after building a product
  with no clear market advantage; the pivot became the current product.
  Self-reported founder narrative.
- [1:00:xx-area, ~38:50] He claims Corgea is "beating competitors making
  $300–400M in revenue" and that one rival sent a board member to a customer
  after losing a bake-off. Anecdotal, unverifiable, no company named.
- [20:01–20:05] AppSec engineer-to-developer support ratio cited as roughly
  1:100–1:200. Stated as industry knowledge, no source given.

## Why it matters here

Useful counter-data for the guide's activation/positioning sections (§01,
§04): a devtool founder explicitly rejecting design partnerships — the
default advice in the category — in favor of forcing early yes/no signal,
and using pilot-conversion rate as a PMF proxy instead of top-of-funnel
metrics. Both are testable practices a smaller team could adopt.

Weakest part for our purposes: this is one founder's retrospective account
with no usage or revenue data attached to any of the tactical claims; the
funding-amount advice is explicitly scoped to Bay Area cybersecurity seed
rounds and may not generalize.
