## What it covers

A solo Jack Bridger episode, not an interview: he reads through and reacts to
an essay by Jason Cohen (founder of WP Engine and SmartBear, both
billion-dollar-plus companies) laying out an eight-step road map to product-market
fit — personal fit, market fit, customer fit, build/ship an SLC ("simple,
lovable, complete", Cohen's term for MVP), marketing and sales, retention-driven
product work, systematic prioritization, and managing founder psychology. This
is general startup strategy, not developer-marketing-specific, but grounded in
two named devtools case studies (WP Engine, SmartBear).

## Claims worth checking

- [41:47] WP Engine reached $1M ARR in 18 months, launching with 30 paying
  customers before the product had a customer dashboard. Self-reported by
  Cohen via his essay, one company.
- [21:47] Cohen's customer-interview outreach for WP Engine: of 50 LinkedIn
  cold requests to WordPress developers offering to pay for an hour of their
  time, 40 agreed and only one asked to be paid; 30 of those interviewees
  eventually became customers. Self-reported, sixteen-plus years old by his
  own account ("a long time ago") — explicitly flagged by Cohen himself as
  possibly not replicable today.
- [37:02–38:14] Counter-case: SmartBear's first product (CodeHistorian) was
  built with no upfront customer interviews and initially misjudged what
  customers wanted; the eventual hit product (CodeCollaborator) came from two
  pivots driven by post-launch customer feedback, not the interview-first
  method. Cohen's own admission that the road map isn't the only path — his
  own company only "halfway followed" it.
- [40:24–40:47] WordPress's share of the top 10M sites went from 11% (unstated
  year, described as when WP Engine started) to 43% (2023) — an essay-cited
  market-growth figure, not independently verified in this episode.
