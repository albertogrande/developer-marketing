## What it covers

Another solo episode: Jack Bridger walks through Jason Cohen's (WP Engine,
SmartBear founder) essay "The Roadmap to Product-Market Fit," an eight-step
sequence — personal fit, market fit, customer fit, build/ship an SLC
("simple, lovable, complete," Cohen's term for MVP), marketing and sales,
retention-driven product work, ruthless prioritization, and managing founder
psychology — illustrated against WP Engine's and SmartBear's own histories.

## Claims worth checking

- [40:47–41:20] WP Engine's first version shipped in 30 hours with no
  customer dashboard, took payment via a stock web form, and survived a
  Hacker News front-page spike with load to spare. Cohen's own account,
  unverified beyond his telling.
- [41:47] WP Engine reached $1M ARR after eighteen months, launching with 30
  paying customers. Self-reported founder figure, not audited.
- [22:21–22:32] Cohen's customer-interview method for WP Engine: 50 LinkedIn
  outreach requests, 40 accepted, only 1 asked to be paid, 30 became
  customers. Self-reported, and Cohen flags it himself as "a long time ago" —
  not a repeatable benchmark.
- [37:02–38:14] Cohen contrasts WP Engine (followed the roadmap end to end)
  against his own SmartBear (skipped customer interviews entirely, pivoted
  twice — CodeHistorian → CodeReviewer → CodeCollaborator — before finding
  the business). Presented by Cohen as evidence that shipping and pivoting
  can substitute for upfront interviews, at the cost of more wasted cycles.

## Why it matters here

Same pattern as the prior week's PMF episode: a well-known practitioner essay
read aloud with light commentary, no new dated fact. The WP Engine and
SmartBear numbers are Cohen's own retelling of his companies' history, useful
as attributed anecdote but not verifiable data — if a guide section ever
cites the WP Engine 30-hour-launch or $1M-ARR-in-18-months figures, they need
attribution to Cohen's essay, not treatment as an independent measurement.

Guide connection: `01-positioning-for-developers` already covers
customer-fit and ICP framing; this episode's "different, not better" framing
of positioning is a possible attributed quote to strengthen that section, not
a new claim.
