The week's biggest number was $60 billion. SpaceX
[closed its all-stock acquisition](/signals#2026-08-16-spacex-closes-cursor-acquisition)
of Cursor maker Anysphere on August 15 — the largest disclosed devtools
acquisition on record, first optioned in an April partnership. Read Cursor's
own announcement for what was actually traded: compute flows in ("the largest
fleet of GPUs in the world," in Cursor's words), and a large base of
professional developers flows out, a ready-made rail for routing Grok models
into working editors. Nobody paid $60 billion for a VS Code fork. They paid
for the rail.

That is the week's throughline, because everyone else was building rails too.
[Last week](/issues/2026-W32) we argued the coding agent became a commodity
and the terms sheet became the pitch. This week showed where the leverage
goes when a layer commoditizes: one layer up, to whoever owns the
distribution.

## The rails multiplied

GitHub [opened an "agent apps" category](/signals#2026-08-14-github-agent-apps-marketplace)
on its Marketplace — nine inaugural partners, Amplitude, LaunchDarkly,
PagerDuty and SonarQube among them, plugged into the same harness that runs
Copilot's own cloud agent. Vercel added
[Exa](/signals#2026-08-14-vercel-exa-agent-marketplace) to its Agent
Marketplace. And the MCP roster stopped being a devtools story:
[MongoDB shipped a fully hosted Atlas MCP server](/signals#2026-08-14-mongodb-atlas-managed-mcp-server)
and [Nutanix shipped one for hybrid-cloud ops](/signals#2026-08-13-nutanix-mcp-server) —
data platforms and enterprise infrastructure incumbents now ship the agent
integration as a product feature, not a hackathon artifact.

Every one of these rails is curated. Someone decides who is listed, and last
week's stranded-integrator timeline put numbers on what that can cost —
a four-month queue at one directory against a 29-day approval at another.
More rails is genuinely good news for a devtool marketer: more places to be
found, and more competition among the gatekeepers. But it also means your
channel mix now has a line item most playbooks don't carry. The play is
unchanged and compounding: inventory which rails your product can be
installed from, dual-list everywhere your buyers' agents actually run, treat
each review queue as a risk with a date on it, and keep an install path you
own so no launch waits on someone else's queue. The
[guide's channels section](/guide/06-channels-and-distribution) carries the
full argument.

## The meter says the model is fungible

Why is the rail worth more than the model? Vercel published the cleanest
answer yet, [from its own AI Gateway routing data](/signals#2026-08-12-vercel-ai-gateway-deepseek-report):
DeepSeek hit 25% of July token volume, past Google's 11% — Google ran nearly
40% in April. Open-weight models went from 11% to 36% of volume in four
months. Three in four high-volume teams changed at least a tenth of their
model mix. Anthropic, meanwhile, held 65% of *spend* on 30% of volume, priced
at 4.4x the field. The caveat is structural — Vercel is both the source and
the router, so this is one gateway's traffic, not the market — but the shape
is hard to argue with: switching models is a one-line change, teams do it
constantly, and premium pricing survives only as long as the capability gap
does.

That is why Z.ai is giving GLM 5.2 away
[free on the gateway through August 27](/signals#2026-08-14-vercel-glm-free-promo) —
when defaults are this loose, a time-boxed free window is a land grab for
default-model share. If you sell anything model-adjacent, assume your users
re-decide the model monthly, and position on what survives a model swap:
the workflow, the data, the integration surface. The rail, again.

## Meta reads the web for itself

The quietest big move: Promptwatch's crawler logs show
[Meta-WebIndexer going from ~2.2% to 37.8%](/signals#2026-08-16-meta-webindexer-crawler-surge)
of tracked AI-crawler requests in under a month — now the heaviest AI crawler
they track, more than one in three requests, coinciding with reports that
Meta is building a web index of its own rather than renting Google's. Whether
or not a Meta search product ships, the crawl is real today, and it says the
crawler population your docs serve is unstable: an allowlist or a robots
policy tuned to last year's bot names quietly goes stale. Re-read your own
logs, not the folklore — the [AEO section](/guide/09-answer-engines-and-aeo)
has the twenty-minute version.
