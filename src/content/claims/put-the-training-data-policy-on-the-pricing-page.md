title: Put your training-data policy on the pricing page
when: Pricing or positioning an AI-assisted developer product, or answering an enterprise security review for one.
do: State whether you train on customer prompts, completions, or code on the pricing page itself — as a term of each tier, not a trust-center FAQ — and decide your answer to data-for-discount pricing before a competitor prices it for you.
why: On 2026-08-05 Meta launched Muse Code with a "contributor" tier at $0.10/$0.20 per million input/output tokens against a $1.25/$4.25 standard tier — roughly 12–21x off in exchange for training rights on prompts and completions. A training-data policy now has a public market rate, which makes silence on yours read as a term you're hiding.
section: 01-positioning-for-developers
tags: [pricing, positioning]
since: Meta Muse Code beta (2026-08-05) — the first coding-agent vendor we've found to price a discount explicitly as a data trade rather than volume or seats
verify: Open Muse Code's pricing — the contributor tier and the ~12–21x spread should still stand — and check whether any incumbent (Anthropic, OpenAI, GitHub) has shipped or publicly ruled out a matching data tier.
