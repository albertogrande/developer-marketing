The argument itself is settled and the house position is on record: report the
*programme* in four auditable numbers, and manage the *people* on craft.
Attribution was always the wrong frame — a developer who read the docs, saw a
talk, and signed up three months later did not arrive through a link, and
building a measurement practice that only counts links measures the wrong
thing precisely.

What is not settled is whether anyone can actually produce the numbers.

## The instrumentation started shipping

The interesting shift in August was that the tooling stopped being something
DevRel teams had to build. GitHub's Copilot impact dashboard added a return-on-
investment section with a named "Passive" seat segment — a vendor voluntarily
showing a buyer how many purchased seats are doing nothing. That is the seller
side handing the customer an uncomfortable number, which is the same mechanic
as the proof-over-adjectives thread, applied to renewals.

Alongside it, two quieter forms of the same idea: docs audits scored against
what the support queue actually asks, and crawler-log data putting AI-answer
presence on a measurable footing — Meta's own indexer went from a rounding
error to 37.8% of observed crawl share inside a month.

## Why it stays open

Because the survey numbers and the tooling numbers disagree. The most recent
wave has 62% of programmes reporting to C-level and 18% able to link revenue,
with 61% saying they cannot demonstrate impact at all. Better instruments have
not yet moved that, and it is entirely possible they will not: the constraint
may be organisational rather than technical.

The other thing worth watching is consolidation. The signal-tooling layer has
been rolling up — three acquisitions in eight months, all on undisclosed
terms. A category whose prices nobody discloses is a category still deciding
what it is worth.
